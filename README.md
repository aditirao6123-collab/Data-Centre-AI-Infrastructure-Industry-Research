# Data-Centre-AI-Infrastructure-Industry-Research
An industry report on the data center sector and how AI is driving its growth. It covers market size, customers, the supply chain and key risks, and ends with an investment recommendation. Sources include the IEA, McKinsey, IDC and company filings.

## What the report covers

**Industry and growth**
Power demand from data centers is projected to roughly double by 2030, and spending by the four largest hyperscalers keeps rising. AI-related demand is growing about four times faster than the rest of the industry.

**Customers and willingness to pay**
I ranked the main customer groups by how much they actually pay today: enterprises first, then AI-native companies, then sovereign AI projects (including the UAE's), and consumers last.

**Contracted vs discretionary spend**
Large contracted backlogs at Microsoft, Oracle, Google Cloud and AWS show that much of the spending is backed by committed customer demand, not just expected demand.

**Supply chain**
I mapped the chain from chip manufacturing and design through site development, construction and colocation to the end customer.

**Replaceability and lock-in**
Switching is hard at every stage, but for different reasons: software dependency for chips (Nvidia's CUDA), trusted relationships for construction, and limited power capacity for colocation.

**Bottlenecks**
The two biggest constraints are chip concentration (Nvidia in design, TSMC in manufacturing) and the slow buildout of power and grid infrastructure.

## Recommendation

Power and cooling make up the largest share of non-IT data center spending and are needed whichever company wins the chip race. Within that, I focused on cooling, where liquid cooling is the fastest growing segment.

Comparing Vertiv and Schneider Electric, I picked **Vertiv** as a long-term investment because of:
- its long history and relationships with hyperscalers and chipmakers
- a $15bn order backlog at the end of 2025
- its partnerships with Nvidia on next-generation AI power and cooling

The report ends with a five-year look at Vertiv's financials, which I then used to build my three-statement model.
